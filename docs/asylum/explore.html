These are annual outcome shares, including administrative closures in the denominator—not cohort success probabilities or substantive-decision recognition rates. Years without a complete, positive, reconciled outcome total are omitted from this percentage chart. Definitions and limitations.

About the country commentary. The gallery profiles describe the fixed 2000-2025 UNHCR extract, not a forecast. Changes compare reported application activity with 2019 where available; peaks are the largest recorded annual totals. Grant rates exclude closures and use complete, positive, reconciled decision totals; they concern decisions made, not outcomes for that year’s arrivals. Procedures, persons/cases mixes and coverage can change. Missing observations remain unavailable rather than being filled with zero. The Grant Rates page separately identifies its refreshed snapshot. Gallery: Top 20 receiving countries

The 20 receiving countries with the largest combined reported application volume, 2000–2025, in the fixed notebook edition. Reported applications and decisions combine persons and cases without conversion; they are not counts of unique people. 1. United States

United States: scale and change. Across 2000–2025, the extract records 6,249,314 applications. The largest annual volume was 1,189,166 in 2023; 2025 recorded 911,952. Applications in 2025 were 189% higher than in 2019 (315,897). In 2025, 597,604 recorded decisions equated to 66 per 100 applications, so recorded output fell below incoming volume. Applications in 2025 were 24% higher than in 2024 (733,912). Applications exceeded recorded decisions in 5 of 5 comparable years during 2021-2025. The leading recorded origins that year were Unknown (282,797), Venezuela (Bolivarian Republic of) (141,616), Mexico (86,001).

In 2025, the substantive protection rate was 23%, across 148,216 grants and refusals; closures accounted for 75% of all recorded decisions. That protection rate was 34.2 percentage points lower than in 2024 (58%).

Source links and downloads

{
  const container = document.createElement("div");
  container.className = "explorer-links";
  if (selected) {
    for (const [dataset, label] of [
      ["asylum-applications", "Current UNHCR applications download"],
      ["asylum-decisions", "Current UNHCR decisions download"]
    ]) {
      const a = document.createElement("a");
      a.textContent = label;
      a.href = core.sourceLink(dataset, selected.iso, data.metadata);
      a.target = "_blank";
      a.rel = "noopener";
      container.append(a);
    }
    const a = document.createElement("a");
    const url = URL.createObjectURL(new Blob([core.csvFor(rows)], {type: "text/csv;charset=utf-8"}));
    a.href = url;
    a.download = selected.iso + "-combined-asylum-chart-data.csv";
    a.textContent = "Download this chart's data (CSV)";
    container.append(a);
    invalidation.then(() => URL.revokeObjectURL(url));
  }
  const snapshot = document.createElement("a");
  snapshot.href = "data/source-snapshot.zip";
  snapshot.textContent = "Download the exact source snapshots (ZIP)";
  container.append(snapshot);
  return container;
}

The CSV contains the selected country’s annual aggregates combining persons- and case-reported values without conversion (unit: P+C); empty fields mean unavailable values. The ZIP contains the full source CSVs and their download provenance. UNHCR’s current downloads can contain later revisions and both reporting units, so they may differ from the stored chart data.

Refresh policy: weekly, plus publication updates. Each refresh validates schema, reporting units, years, non-negative counts, and aggregation totals. A failed refresh does not replace the published dataset. Download time is not the same as UNHCR’s publication date or confirmation that a year is complete.
