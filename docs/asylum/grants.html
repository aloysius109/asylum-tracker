Grant Rates

Overall decision outcomes and annual percentages for receiving countries.

Explore the overall decision-outcome pies for the Top 20 receiving countries, or choose any receiving country below for its overall and annual figures.

Two denominators, two questions. Pie slices show refugee status, other protection, rejection, and closure as percentages of all recorded decisions. The refugee recognition rate is refugee status divided by substantive decisions (refugee status + other protection + rejection). The total protection rate includes refugee status and other protection in its numerator. Both substantive rates exclude administrative closures.

Overall percentages divide summed outcomes by summed decision counts, rather than averaging annual percentages. Only years with complete, positive, reconciled decision totals contribute; unavailable values are not zero. Percentages describe decisions, not the eventual success of an application cohort.

Original persons- and case-reported counts are combined without conversion, as on the Country Explorer. Different unit mixes, procedures, and reporting coverage affect comparisons. These are exploratory rates, not harmonised person-based estimates. Methods and definitions.

How protection rates differ and change

An overall rate mixes policy, procedure and the claims being decided. A change can arise because the nationalities or types of claims changed, because older claims were decided, or because decision practices changed. The descriptive comparisons below establish differences, not their causes. Closures are excluded from substantive rates and shown separately on this page.

These are the five largest absolute 2024–2025 changes among the fixed Top 20 receiving countries with complete, reconciled annual totals and at least 500 substantive decisions in both years. Closures: changes over time and differences between countries

Closure share = otherwise-closed decisions ÷ all recorded decisions. Closures are processing outcomes without a substantive grant or refusal. They deserve a separate view from recognition rates.

UNHCR’s “otherwise closed” category can include withdrawal and abandonment, but also inadmissibility, death, or transfers under European procedures. It does not identify how many claimants abandoned a claim while remaining in-country. Differences can reflect procedure and reporting as well as claimant behaviour. UNHCR reporting definitions.
