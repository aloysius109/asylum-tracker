Asylum under pressure

The global picture, the processing gap, and the countries keeping pace.

Author

Kathryn Hopkins

Report summary · Saved data through 2025

The world recorded far more asylum applications in 2025 than in 2019, while the observed unresolved caseload more than doubled. Some receiving countries have recently matched incoming volume with decisions; others show repeated processing shortfalls.

9.0m asylum seekers pending at end-2025 8,998,097 persons · observed UNHCR stock

3.3m reported applications in 2025 3,343,203 application events · mixed reporting units

85 decisions per 100 applications in 2025 2.8m recorded decisions · closures included

The recorded 2025 flow gap was 507,016: that many more applications than decisions were reported. This is an imbalance between annual flows, not an estimate of the backlog. The pending headline above comes from a separate observed population series.

The main findings

  1. High demand, despite a retreat from the peak. Applications in 2025 were 49% above 2019, but 2.3% below 2024. The recorded peak was 3.8m in 2023. Recent growth has not been uninterrupted. Explore the worldwide trends.
  2. The unresolved stock has grown faster. The observed asylum-seeker stock rose 117% since 2019, and 7.7% from 2024 to 2025. It is a persons-based population measure, distinct from mixed-unit application events. See the stock series.
  3. Processing pressure is uneven. Some receiving countries have recorded enough output to match recent inflow; others have repeated shortfalls. Period totals, annual variation and closures all matter. Read the processing assessment.
  4. Protection rates differ sharply. The same recorded nationality can have very different aggregate outcomes in different receiving countries, and rates can change substantially over time. Different claim circumstances, procedures and timing prevent interpreting these as individual chances or isolated policy effects. Compare grant rates and recent changes.

Who appears to be keeping pace?

Stronger recorded flow balances

Germany (102), Sweden (117), Switzerland (102).

The figures are decisions per 100 applications across 2021–2025. These countries also met or exceeded 100 in the recent three-year period and in 2025. This supports a finding of stronger recorded flow balance, not that their pending caseloads have been cleared.

Repeated shortfalls

United States (40), Italy (61), Canada (59), Egypt (15), Belgium (78), Mexico (65), Kenya (31), Netherlands (71).

Each had fewer decisions than applications in at least four of five years, with aggregate output also below inflow in 2021–2025 and 2023–2025. A shortfall signals pressure; it does not directly measure waiting time or backlog growth.

Recent catch-up: United Kingdom (92), Austria (97). Their 2023–2025 output exceeded inflow, despite a five-year shortfall. Close to balance: the Russian Federation. Mixed patterns: France’s period balance is stronger than its latest-year balance; Peru’s high ratio is dominated by closures. South Africa and Türkiye have incomplete recent flow coverage. Scope of this summary. Application and decision counts describe the fixed 2000–2025 UNHCR extract, combining persons and cases without conversion, across reported procedures and application types. They are reported activities, not harmonised counts of unique people. Coverage can change. Latest-year figures need not be complete worldwide returns. Pending stock is reported separately in persons. The interactive explorers identify their own saved refresh dates. This report describes observed patterns; it does not predict inevitable future growth or establish policy causes. Methods & sources.
