UK asylum: comparing the sources

Home Office official statistics alongside UNHCR first-instance records.

This page brings the UK’s official statistics into the report without rerunning the notebook. The comparison uses calendar years 2010–2025, with newer UK-only information shown separately.

What the UK trends show

The Home Office calendar-year series records 101,911 people claiming asylum in 2025, compared with 45,537 in 2019 (124% higher). In 2025 it recorded 135,740 initial decisions. Initial decision output exceeded that year’s claims, but those decisions include claims lodged earlier; this is not the fraction of 2025 applicants who were processed.

The published main-applicant initial grant rate was 42% in 2025, versus 52% in 2019. This official rate has its own applicant scope: it should not be reconstructed from the all-people grant and decision counts. The separately dated small-boat rates below concern a specific arrival group, rather than everyone claiming asylum in the UK.

Cross-country context uses the broader UNHCR extract. On that common reported-activity basis, the UK recorded 168,185 applications and 194,877 decisions in 2025, or 116 decisions per 100 applications. The corresponding ratios were Germany (124), France (98), Canada (72). These contrasts show flows relative to demand, not staff efficiency or waiting times. They combine persons and cases and different procedure mixes; the first-instance persons-only comparison below has a deliberately narrower scope.

Match the period and the measure first

This comparison deliberately uses UNHCR first-instance (FI), persons-reported (P) rows for the UK, rather than the combined-unit, all-procedure totals elsewhere in the report. Appeals and other stages are excluded. The Home Office separates initial decisions from withdrawals and other administrative outcomes; the UNHCR comparison decision count therefore sums refugee recognition, complementary protection, and rejection, excluding closures.

Measure Home Office UNHCR comparison
Inflows People claiming asylum, including dependants First-instance application records reported as persons
Substantive decision volume Initial grants and refusals, including dependants First-instance recognised + other protection + rejected, reported as persons
Grant percentage Published rate for main applicants: protection or other leave, excluding withdrawals and administrative outcomes (Recognised + complementary protection) / (recognised + complementary protection + rejected), in persons-reported FI records
Pending headline People awaiting an initial decision at the reporting date The worldwide stock elsewhere in the report has a different geographic and procedural scope

The rate definitions still differ: applicant coverage and the treatment of other leave are not fully harmonised. Counts can differ through revisions, reporting scope, and timing. Differences are a prompt to investigate, not evidence that either source is wrong. No gap between these lines is labelled an error or interpreted as a policy effect.

Application and substantive decision volumes

Each series counts activity in the year of the event. Decisions may relate to claims made earlier; these are not matched application cohorts. Unlike the international combined-unit charts, this comparison retains a persons-only scope to avoid adding case counts to people.

Grant proportions, with closures removed

The published UK rate is taken directly from the official table. It is not reconstructed by dividing grants including dependants by decisions including dependants. That would answer a different question. The UNHCR rate is calculated from its own first-instance outcome counts, with no closure outcomes in the denominator.

Small-boat asylum grant rates by nationality

The Home Office publishes linked asylum outcomes for people recorded arriving by small boat, rather than requiring us to infer rates from unrelated arrival and decision totals. The grant measure here is grants of protection or other leave ÷ (grants + refusals), for main applicants. Withdrawals, administrative outcomes and pending claims are excluded from that denominator, but shown separately in the cohort table.

Both views below concern initial decisions, not final outcomes after appeals or a prediction of an individual’s chance of asylum. Official Home Office tables and definitions.

Rwanda and the former UK asylum partnership

What the UK proposed—and what happened

The UK and Rwanda announced their Migration and Economic Development Partnership in April 2022. It proposed relocating certain asylum seekers from the UK to Rwanda, where their protection claims would be considered. The December 2023 treaty developed the asylum-partnership arrangements. Home Office background, UK–Rwanda asylum-partnership statement.

The Home Office discontinued the policy in July 2024. This is historical context, not a current UK processing programme. Home Office confirmation.

In its December 2024 account, the Home Office reported that no asylum seekers had been sent to Rwanda under the policy; it separately reported four volunteers. Those volunteers should not be treated as asylum seekers whose claims were processed through the proposed relocation scheme. Official account.

Rwanda’s own reported asylum activity

These figures describe Rwanda’s reported asylum system, not UK-to-Rwanda transfers. They do not measure the partnership’s results or establish how Rwanda would have decided relocated UK claims. The extract has no identifier isolating a UK-relocation cohort, and the aggregate outcomes cannot establish future capacity or individual chances of protection.

Rwanda in context: a smaller and unevenly reported system

Rwanda recorded 1,835 applications and 777 decisions in 2025. Application volume was below the 12,899 recorded in 2023, rather than following an uninterrupted upward trend. In 2025, decisions amounted to 42 per 100 applications, compared with 116 in the UK on the same broad UNHCR reported-activity basis. A low annual ratio can reflect the timing of decisions or reporting, as well as an imbalance between inflow and output; it is not a measured processing time or an observed backlog.

All 777 recorded Rwanda decisions in 2025 were refugee-status recognitions, yielding a 100% substantive protection rate in this extract. For context, the same broad extract records 2025 substantive protection rates of United Kingdom (40%; 156,735 substantive decisions), Germany (34%; 279,201 substantive decisions), France (37%; 206,819 substantive decisions). These are aggregate contrasts, not a matched-origin comparison. That describes the reported decisions, not every applicant’s eventual outcome. The origin cards below help put the rate in context: a different claimant mix from the UK, a smaller decision denominator and different procedures prevent treating the two aggregate rates as equivalent chances of success. Nor does this domestic activity establish how Rwanda would have processed people relocated from the UK under the former partnership.
